The house pastels, light and dark

Each family has the same roles: fill (the house pastel, exact), ink (text and icons on it), strong (solid buttons, with the on label), graphic (rings and marks), and the tile gradient from fill to its saturated end (its glyph is an icon, checked at 3:1 against the saturated end). Light uses Hearth's white card; dark uses Midnight's, where the pastel becomes the glowing foreground on a deep tint. Graphite is the neutral family (the TV, signed-out states); success, warning and danger are the semantic families.

Neutrals and surfaces, every palette

Grouped iOS structure: a page, white (or raised dark) cards, wells. The five named palettes plus Graphite, the proposed iOS near-black (decision D2). Text, secondary and tertiary text are checked against the card and the well.

Type scale

iOS Dynamic Type roles in the system face (SF Pro on Apple devices; never embedded), bold large titles (decision D6), rounded numerals, and a floor of 11 px. The iPad tier multiplies by 1.12 on touch screens 744 px and wider; kid mode is rounded everywhere with a 16 px floor; the TV keeps today's sizes until the Phase 6 re-layout turns on the 10-foot scale (decision D16).

Glass over busy content

Liquid Glass is for the navigation and control layer only: bars, sheets, pills and floating buttons. Content cards stay solid. The person's colour is caught faintly in the pane. Shown over the park map's illustrated art, the busiest picture in the hub, in light, dark, and with Reduce Transparency (solid panes, no blur) — the in-app switch Safari needs (decision D11).

App tiles at phone and iPad density

iOS home-screen density: 60 px icons in four columns on the phone, 76 px in six on the iPad, corners at 22.5 % of the icon (decision D9), each tile a pastel-to-saturated gradient within one hue with the ink glyph (decision D5 gives each app its hue). The glyphs are today's icons; the proposal moves them to one open-source set (Lucide, ISC).
