The house pastels, light and dark

Each family has the same roles: fill (the house pastel, exact), ink (text and icons on it), strong (solid buttons, with the on label), graphic (rings and marks), and the tile gradient from fill to its saturated end (its glyph is an icon, checked at 3:1 against the saturated end). Light uses Hearth's white card; dark uses Midnight's, where the pastel becomes the glowing foreground on a deep tint (decision D15). There are three groups: the eight people's pastels, then the nine app colours, which sit between them and belong to no person, then graphite and the status colours.

The four glass levels

Glass is for the navigation and control layer only: bars, sheets, pills and floating buttons. Content cards stay solid. The person's colour is caught faintly in the pane. Each person picks a level in Me → Appearance (decisions D8 and D11); Frosted is the default. The stages sit over the park map's illustrated art, the busiest picture in the hub. Each stage is the page itself with the level set on <html> as the bootstrap sets it, so the token file's own rules paint it. The figures under each stage are read from that page.

  • Clear is under 50 % opaque. Current is today's look. Both are see-through, so text and icons on them get a thin outline in the card's colour, which keeps them readable over a photo. Judge whether that outline looks right: strokes read slightly heavier, and in dark slightly embossed. At 11 px the letters' counters narrow, and a selected tab's colour reads mostly as outline. Measured as rendered by audits/tools/phase5/halo-check.mjs (the 10th percentile of the ring just outside each glyph, in all six palettes), Clear over a black or white photo reads at least text 10.28:1, secondary text 7.89:1 and icons 7.05:1. Tertiary text reads at least 5.93:1 over mid grey (#767676), but only about 1.3:1 over a photo, so tertiary text is never allowed on glass over a photo.
  • Frosted (78-80 % pills, 90 % bars) needs no outline.
  • Solid is Reduce Transparency: opaque panes and no blur. Safari cannot report the iOS setting, so this switch is the way to get it on the iPad and iPhone.

App tiles at phone and iPad density

iOS home-screen density: 60 px icons in four columns on the phone, 76 px in six on the iPad, corners at 22.5 % of the icon (decision D9). Each tile is a gradient from pastel to saturated within the app's own colour, with the ink glyph. The nine app colours are their own, never a person's (D5): Timer coral, Prayer apricot, Kid Verse honey, Verses pistachio, the Larder leaf, Tally seafoam, the park map lagoon, F260 cornflower, the build guide orchid. The glyphs are today's icons; the proposal moves them to one open-source set (Lucide, ISC).

Me → Admin → Edit: the colour picker

Batch 2a (GAP-ACCENT-1). Here the admin is recolouring Mae. All 18 colours are offered: the people's nine first, then the nine app colours, each named with its app. A colour someone already has, one an app uses, or one a colour-blind viewer would confuse with another person's gets a note, not a block; the admin decides. The nine people's colours are kept apart for colour-blind viewers by the gate; the app colours are not, so the picker checks.
